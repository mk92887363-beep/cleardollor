How your mortgage payment is calculated

Your monthly payment is set by three things: the amount you borrow, the interest rate and the length of the loan. A lender spreads the loan across every month of the term so each payment is the same size.

Why the first years feel slow

Early payments are mostly interest, because interest is charged on the full balance. Each month a little more goes to principal, so the balance falls faster over time. That is why a 30-year loan costs much more in total interest than a 15-year loan, even though the monthly payment is lower.

What the calculator leaves out

Property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA dues and private mortgage insurance (usually required with less than 20% down) are added on top. Ask your lender for a full estimate before you decide what you can afford.

Official guidance: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, Owning a Home.

Debt snowball or avalanche?

Both methods work the same way: pay the minimum on every debt, then put all extra money toward one target debt until it is gone.

Avalanche

Target the debt with the highest interest rate first. This costs the least interest overall.

Snowball

Target the smallest balance first. You pay interest slightly longer, but clearing whole debts early keeps many people motivated.

Which should you pick?

If you will stick with either one, the avalanche saves more money. If you need early wins to keep going, the snowball is a fine choice. Use the debt payoff calculator to see how an extra $50 a month changes your timeline.
